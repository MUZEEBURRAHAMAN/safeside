# SafeSide — UI/UX Audit (2026-07-11)

> 12 screens audited in parallel vs the Yuka / Ivy / Oasis clean-UI benchmark, brand locked to `DESIGN_SYSTEM_V3`. Generated by the per-screen audit workflow.

**Totals:** 73 findings — 🔴 3 critical · 🟠 21 high · 🟡 32 medium · ⚪ 17 low

## Cross-screen themes (fix once, help everywhere)
- **Flat cards** — `surfaceCard` has no shadow; v3 mandates soft-shadow floating cards (9 screens).
- **Missing Space Grotesk** on headers/section titles — brand face only appears on the score number (8 screens).
- **Camera permission fires prematurely** over onboarding + a bare gray scan canvas — no priming (2 screens, both CRITICAL).
- **Empty/dead-end states** are boxy left-aligned cards, not calm centered Oasis-style states (11 screens).
- **Colored text fails AA contrast** — band tints used as small text (4 screens, 1 CRITICAL).
- **Green-CTA clutter** — multiple full-green pills per view kill green scarcity (7 screens).

## Scanner + overlay
**Top win:** Add a benefit-first camera-permission priming screen for the .notDetermined state (on-brand dark hero + concrete payoff line + "Turn on camera" pill + "Not now"), and only call requestAccess on the affirmative tap — so the one-shot iOS system prompt never fires over today's blank gray canvas.

- 🔴 **CRITICAL · empty-state** — On first entry to Scan the iOS system camera prompt fires immediately over a bare, un-branded system-gray canvas — the screenshot shows only a black notch/status bar, the word "Scan", and Apple's dialog on flat gray. There is no priming/explainer screen. CameraAvailability.current returns .ready for BOTH .authorized and .notDetermined, so ScanScreen.content routes straight to scannerBody→ScannerView, whose CameraViewController calls AVCaptureDevice.requestAccess on appear (configureSessionIfNeeded). The user meets a permission decision with zero brand, zero context, and a blank backdrop.
  - **Why:** This is a hard miss of teardown STEAL #11 — benefit-first permission priming with concrete payoff + "Not now" (Oasis 04/07 in competitor-04/07, Ivy 58). The iOS camera prompt is a ONE-SHOT: if the user taps "Don't Allow" only a Settings trip can recover it, and the app drops to the permissionDeniedView dead-ish state. Competitors explain value first and trigger the OS prompt only on an affirmative tap, materially raising grant rate. A blank gray screen behind the dialog also violates DESIGN_SYSTEM_V3 §5.9 (the scan is the dark brand moment) — it reads as unfinished, the opposite of the calm, premium Oasis/Ivy bar.
  - **Fix:** Split CameraAvailability into a .needsPriming case for .notDetermined (distinct from .authorized→.ready). For .needsPriming, render a full-bleed Theme.ink dark-hero priming view: Space Grotesk headline (e.g. "See what's really inside"), a one-line concrete payoff, a full-pill greenDeep "Turn on camera" primary that calls AVCaptureDevice.requestAccess(for:.video){…} then flips availability, and a quiet "Not now" text button. Keep the current auto-prompt only as a fallback. This also removes the raw-gray backdrop because the priming view owns the background. `ScannerView.swift:905`
- 🟠 **HIGH · interaction** — The scan-confirmation feedback ("Reading the barcode…" spinner) is pinned to the very BOTTOM of the screen: phaseBanner is attached with .overlay(alignment: .bottom) and labeledSpinner adds .padding(.bottom, Theme.Space.s6). But the reticle that just locked sits at the vertical center (ScanOverlay positions it at proxy.size center). So at the moment of capture the eyes are on the mid-screen locked bracket while the confirmation text appears a screen-height away at the bottom.
  - **Why:** Teardown STEAL #13 is explicit: an "Identifying" pill floats directly over/above the locked reticle, non-blocking (Oasis 17, competitor-17). Co-locating the confirmation with the thing that changed is what makes the scan feel instant and connected; separating them by the full screen height breaks the cause→effect read and feels laggy even when it isn't.
  - **Fix:** Move the .lookingUp / .capturingLabel loading pill out of the bottom banner and into ScanOverlay, positioned just above the reticle center (mirror instructionLabel's .position(x: center.x, y: center.y - reticleSize/2 - Theme.Space.s6)). Keep only the actionable banners (needsOCR/labelNotFound/error) at the bottom. Reuse Theme.forest capsule styling so it matches the instruction pill. `ScannerView.swift:1031`
- 🟡 **MEDIUM · hierarchy** — The screen keeps a standard inline navigation bar with the title "Scan" (navigationTitle("Scan") + .navigationBarTitleDisplayMode(.inline)) sitting over the immersive camera feed (scannerBody uses .ignoresSafeArea). Rendered as default dark ink text, "Scan" lands on a bright/variable camera background with no plate behind it — legibility swings with whatever the camera sees, and the OS chrome fights the dark brand moment.
  - **Why:** Yuka/Oasis/Ivy scan screens are chrome-minimal immersive viewfinders (Oasis shows only a tiny centered wordmark over the feed, competitor-17). DESIGN_SYSTEM_V3 §5.9 defines Scan as the dark hero with minimal chrome; a system title bar with unplated dark text is both an accessibility contrast risk (WCAG §7) and a break in immersion.
  - **Fix:** On the live-scan (.ready) state, hide the nav bar (.toolbar(.hidden, for: .navigationBar) / .navigationBarHidden(true)) so the viewfinder is edge-to-edge; if a label is wanted, drop it into the existing instruction pill treatment (Theme.forest capsule, Theme.onGreen) rather than the system title. Keep the standard nav bar only for the light permission/unavailable states. `ScannerView.swift:947`
- 🟡 **MEDIUM · consistency** — The entire control cluster (zoom / gallery / torch) and the instruction pill are gated on phase == .scanning (showsControlCluster / showsInstruction). The instant phase flips to .needsOCR, .labelNotFound, or .error, all aiming affordances vanish and the reticle drops its lock styling back to the resting bracket, while a banner covers the bottom. The scanner is still running underneath, so the user is invited to "Try again" / re-aim — but with no torch to fix the low light that likely caused the failure and no gallery escape hatch.
  - **Why:** "Never a dead-end" (CLAUDE.md principle 4) means the recovery states should keep the tools that fix the problem. labelNotFound literally says "Try again in better light" yet simultaneously removes the torch button that provides better light — a self-contradicting affordance loss. Oasis/Ivy keep the viewfinder controls persistent across identify/retry.
  - **Fix:** Keep controlCluster visible for the re-aimable phases (.scanning, .needsOCR, .labelNotFound, .error) and only hide it during the brief blocking .lookingUp/.capturingLabel moments. Gate it on a small computed set rather than == .scanning, and ensure the torch button in particular survives into labelNotFound. `ScanOverlay.swift:55`
- 🟡 **MEDIUM · typography** — The zoom button label ("1×"/"2×"/"3×", or a clamped "2.5×") uses .font(.subheadline.weight(.semibold)) with default proportional figures in a fixed 44×44 frame. As the user cycles levels the digits are proportional, so a clamped decimal like "2.5×" changes glyph metrics and the number visibly shifts/jitters against the neighboring gallery/torch glyphs; even single digits can wobble on the baseline between taps.
  - **Why:** The make-interfaces-feel-better gate requires tabular/monospaced figures for any number that changes in place, so it updates without reflow or optical jitter — this is exactly a value that mutates on every tap. Yuka/Oasis camera controls read rock-steady; a jittering zoom readout undercuts the calm-premium feel.
  - **Fix:** Add .monospacedDigit() (or .font(.system(.subheadline, design: .default).monospacedDigit().weight(.semibold))) to the zoom Text, so "1×/2×/3×/2.5×" all occupy identical digit widths and the label stays optically pinned in its 44pt slot. `ScanOverlay.swift:225`
- ⚪ **LOW · consistency** — topBar (ScanOverlay.swift 111–119) and the showsInstruction computed property (line 54) are dead code — the body never references topBar; the instruction pill is rendered directly via instructionLabel gated on showsControlCluster. showsInstruction is only read by the unused topBar.
  - **Why:** Two ways to place the instruction (one live, one orphaned) invite drift — a future edit to topBar/showsInstruction would silently do nothing, and it muddies an otherwise clean overlay. Not user-visible, but it's the kind of residue that hides real bugs.
  - **Fix:** Delete the topBar computed view and the showsInstruction property; keep the single live path (instructionLabel positioned below the reticle via showsControlCluster). `ScanOverlay.swift:111`

## Onboarding
**Top win:** Stop the raw iOS camera-permission dialog from firing over onboarding step 1: the scanner tab is mounting behind the onboarding cover. Present OnboardingView instead of RootTabView (not layered over it) so the camera session never initializes during onboarding, and add an in-flow priming step (concrete payoff + "Not now") before any system permission dialog.

- 🔴 **CRITICAL · interaction** — The screenshot shows the raw iOS 'SafeSide would like to access the Camera' system dialog covering onboarding step 1. RootTabView (which contains the Scan tab / ScannerView camera session) is the base view and OnboardingView is layered on top via .fullScreenCover (FoodScannerApp.swift:114 & 122), so the TabView instantiates the scanner and the AVCaptureSession initializes behind the cover — firing the permission prompt over the very first question, before any context or payoff.
  - **Why:** This is the exact cold-permission anti-pattern the teardown flags: STEAL #11 (Oasis 04/07, Ivy 58) primes the camera ask INSIDE a benefit-framed screen with a concrete payoff and a 'Not now' out, never a raw dialog on cold open. A system prompt interrupting step 1 both breaks the calm first impression and tanks grant rates. It also violates the never-a-dead-end / calm principles.
  - **Fix:** Gate the scanner so its camera never initializes during onboarding: present OnboardingView INSTEAD of RootTabView (e.g. `if showOnboarding { OnboardingView {…} } else { appBody }`) rather than as a fullScreenCover over it, OR start the AVCaptureSession in ScanScreen/ScannerView only when the Scan tab is actually visible AND `!showOnboarding`. Then add a dedicated priming step in OnboardingView (payoff line + 'Enable camera' / 'Not now') that requests access, so the user sees the value before the system dialog. `FoodScannerApp.swift:122`
- 🟠 **HIGH · hierarchy** — The step's outer VStack uses one uniform `Theme.Space.s5` (24pt) gap between the hero title, the subline, the option cards, and the 'Skip this question' link (OnboardingView.swift:70). Every element is equally spaced, so the subline floats midway between title and options instead of reading as part of the header — visible in the screenshot as a large, undifferentiated gap under 'What brings you here?'.
  - **Why:** Yuka/Ivy/Oasis group the title + supporting line tightly (Law of Proximity) then break to content, which is what makes the hierarchy read at a glance. Uniform spacing flattens the grouping and wastes vertical rhythm the refs use to feel premium and scannable.
  - **Fix:** Wrap the title + subline in their own `VStack(alignment: .leading, spacing: Theme.Space.s2)` header group, then set the outer VStack spacing to `Theme.Space.s6` (32) so the break before the option cards is clearly larger than the title→subline gap. `OnboardingView.swift:70`
- 🟡 **MEDIUM · consistency** — There are three ways to make no choice on one screen: the inline 'Skip this question' (OnboardingView.swift:84), the toolbar 'Skip for now' (line 107), and 'Continue' (line 277) which advances even with nothing selected on single-select steps. On step 1 the user sees both a quiet inline skip and a quiet toolbar skip whose scopes (skip-this-step vs end-onboarding) aren't distinguishable from their labels alone.
  - **Why:** The teardown's Oasis pattern is ONE always-visible Skip with an equal-weight primary (AVOID #11). Two same-styled, same-weight 'Skip…' affordances plus an empty-Continue path is redundant and momentarily ambiguous — friction on a flow that's meant to feel effortless.
  - **Fix:** Keep the quiet toolbar 'Skip for now' (skip-all) as the single global exit, and only render the inline 'Skip this question' on steps that genuinely can't be 'continued' empty, or relabel it to 'Skip →' with a caption clarifying it's per-question. Ensure the two never read as duplicate controls. `OnboardingView.swift:84`
- 🟡 **MEDIUM · component** — The pinned footer with the Continue pill shares `Theme.canvas` with the scroll region and has no top hairline, scrim, or shadow (OnboardingView.swift:266-288). On steps with enough options to scroll, the last option card butts directly against the Continue button with no boundary, so the CTA doesn't read as a persistent action bar.
  - **Why:** The refs (and the teardown's 'sticky bottom pill CTA' cue) keep the persistent action visually separated from scrolling content so it reads as pinned and content clearly passes beneath it. A borderless same-color footer looks like content, weakening the 'one obvious primary action' the whole screen is built around.
  - **Fix:** Add a 1px `Theme.border` top divider or a short top-edge scrim/`.shadow(y: -2, low opacity)` to the footer container so it reads as a distinct action bar over the canvas, matching the card-separation language used elsewhere. `OnboardingView.swift:266`
- ⚪ **LOW · typography** — Numeric UI uses proportional digits: the stepper value (OnboardingView.swift:249-251) and 'Step N of 8' (line 120). When a stepper increments across a digit-width change (e.g. 9→10) or the step counter updates, glyph widths shift and the row/label jitters.
  - **Why:** The make-interfaces polish gate calls for tabular/monospaced digits on any value that changes in place, so numbers don't reflow. It's a cheap, brand-safe micro-polish that separates a considered app from a default one.
  - **Fix:** Add `.monospacedDigit()` to the stepper value Text (line 250) and the 'Step N of 8' Text (line 120). No token or brand change needed. `OnboardingView.swift:249`
- ⚪ **LOW · density** — 'Step 1 of 8' announces an 8-step flow at the very first screen (Step enum, OnboardingView.swift:28-29). Several steps carry a single thin decision (e.g. budget, cook time), so the counter signals more friction than the compact interest picker Oasis uses.
  - **Why:** For a calm, guest-first, fully-optional onboarding, an upfront '1 of 8' primes the user for a long form and undercuts the 'a few quick questions' promise in the subline — the opposite of the effortless feel the teardown credits to Oasis's onboarding.
  - **Fix:** Consider merging thin steps (e.g. fold budget into the goal step, combine household + health) to land ~4-5 steps, or de-emphasize the total (show progress bar without the explicit '/8' until later). Keep every step skippable. `OnboardingView.swift:28`

## Result (product score / trust moment)
**Top win:** Give the hero score ring a plain-language verdict word. Right now the flagship trust element shows only "51 / 100" plus an amber arc — no word telling the user what 51 means. Oasis's benchmark ring pairs number + word ("73/100 Good"). Add the band label with a band-tinted SF Symbol beside/under the ring so the verdict reads in one glance and isn't carried by an ambiguous number+color alone.

- 🔴 **CRITICAL · accessibility** — The severity words in the tri-metric tiles ('Strong', 'Moderate', 'Limited') and the Watch-outs/Benefits meter tier words ('moderate') are rendered in the raw band tints as tiny bold text on white. In the screenshot 'Strong' (scoreHigh #1FC24D) and 'Moderate'/'moderate' (scoreMid #C9A227) are the low-contrast amber/green words. Measured contrast: scoreMid 2.42:1, scoreHigh 2.36:1 — both fail WCAG AA (needs 4.5:1 for this small text). Ironically Theme+Result.swift already documents this exact failure and defines warningTextOnLight for it, but the tiles don't use it.
  - **Why:** WCAG 2.1 AA is a non-negotiable project principle (#6) and the design system §7 says 'AA contrast (verify green fills)'. Yuka/Oasis keep their verdict words in high-contrast ink and let the ring/bar carry the color — the word must be legible on its own. Color-as-text at these tints is the most common a11y miss and it's on the flagship screen.
  - **Fix:** Stop coloring the WORD text with the band tint. Set TriMetricTile's word (ResultComponents.swift:290) and MeterRowView's tier (line 1210) to Theme.textPrimary (17.9:1) — or Theme.ResultScreen.warningTextOnLight (5.06:1) for the amber family. Keep the band tint on the adjacent ProgressView bar and the icon so the color signal (word + bar + icon) stays redundant and calm, just AA-legible. No new colors — both tokens already exist. `ResultComponents.swift:290`
- 🟠 **HIGH · hierarchy** — The hero score ring shows only '51' and '/ 100' inside it and an amber arc — there is NO plain-language verdict word anywhere near it. ScoreBadge's VStack renders number + '/100' only; band.label lives solely in the accessibilityLabel. The overall verdict (what 51 actually means) is never stated in words on screen; the per-factor words below don't summarize the whole product.
  - **Why:** This is the trust moment. The Oasis benchmark ring (competitor-11) pairs number + word ('73/100' with 'Good' beneath), and DESIGN_SYSTEM_V3 §5.3 explicitly specifies 'big number in a band-tinted ring, band word + icon beside it' with 'three redundant signals — number + word + arc'. Only two are present, so a sighted user decodes the verdict from a number and an amber color — exactly the black-box ambiguity the product is trying to beat.
  - **Fix:** In identityHeader (ProductView.swift:283-293), add a band-word line next to the ring: Text(band.label) in the band tint (AA-safe: clay passes; for mid use warningTextOnLight) with a small band SF Symbol, or add a third Text(band.label) line inside ScoreBadge's VStack (ScoreBadge.swift:79) under '/ 100'. Use the neutral tier wording from COPY_DECK (e.g. 'Fair'/'Moderate'), never 'good/bad'. `ScoreBadge.swift:79`
- 🟡 **MEDIUM · typography** — The product name uses system .title2.bold (ProductView.swift:302) and every section header ('Watch-outs', 'Benefits', 'Why this score', 'What's inside', 'Allergens', 'Sources') uses system .title3.weight(.semibold). Only the score number uses Space Grotesk (Font.display). So the screen's whole heading layer is generic SF Pro.
  - **Why:** DESIGN_SYSTEM_V3 §3 and §8.2 mandate 'Space Grotesk on all hero/score/section headlines' — it is the locked brand's typographic signature and a key differentiator from the SF-Pro competitors. Right now the brand voice appears once (the number) and the rest reads like a default iOS list, flattening the confident, distinctive hierarchy Yuka/Oasis get from a strong display face.
  - **Fix:** Route the product name to Font.display (e.g. Font.display(26, weight: .bold, relativeTo: .title2)) at ProductView.swift:302, and give section titles a shared Space Grotesk h3 style (Font.display ~18-20 semibold) instead of .title3 across ResultComponents.swift (e.g. MetersSection:1240, WhyScoreSection:428, ingredientsSection:439). The Font.display helper already exists and is used by ScoreBadge. `ProductView.swift:302`
- 🟡 **MEDIUM · consistency** — Severity/tier words are cased inconsistently within one viewport: the tri-metric tiles show 'Limited', 'Strong', 'Moderate' (capitalized), while the Watch-outs meters two rows below show 'moderate', 'moderate' (lowercase, rendered verbatim from row.tier at MeterRowView line 1208). Same concept (a severity word), same screen, different casing.
  - **Why:** Design-teardown STEAL #8/#9 wants consistent severity-word + status rows; the inconsistency reads as unpolished and undermines the calm, deliberate feel that separates Oasis from the 'badge soup' apps. Small casing mismatches are exactly the optical-polish gate items the make-interfaces-feel-better skill flags.
  - **Fix:** Display-capitalize the tier for rendering only — Text(row.tier.capitalized) at ResultComponents.swift:1208 — so it matches the tri-metric words. Keep the raw backend value for the accessibilityLabel/analytics (do not mutate the model). Confirm the tri-metric 'word' vocabulary and the meter tier vocabulary don't collide oddly (e.g. both 'Moderate'). `ResultComponents.swift:1208`
- ⚪ **LOW · spacing** — resultContent stacks all sections with VStack spacing Theme.Space.s5 (24pt). DESIGN_SYSTEM_V3 §4 specifies 'Section gap 28-32'. At 24pt the identity block, tri-metric row, Watch-outs, Benefits and Why-this-score run a touch tight for a screen this dense.
  - **Why:** Generous, consistent section rhythm is called out as 'part of the premium feel' (§4) and is what makes Oasis feel calm despite showing a lot. A slightly larger, on-spec section gap improves scannability and separates the stacked transparency sections without adding chrome.
  - **Fix:** Change the resultContent VStack spacing from Theme.Space.s5 (24) to Theme.Space.s6 (32) at ProductView.swift:200 to match the 28-32 section-gap spec; keep the 20pt (s45/screen) horizontal padding unchanged. `ProductView.swift:200`

## Compare
**Top win:** Make the side-by-side comparison legible: label each of the two stacked bars with its product (not just position) and mark the winning bar with a "Higher" word-tag, so a user can actually tell which product won each metric at a glance instead of decoding a faint color wash and remembering "top bar = left column."

- 🟠 **HIGH · hierarchy** — In the standard side-by-side layout each metric card stacks two identical anonymous bars (product A above product B) with only a value like 94/100 vs 100/100. Nothing on the bar says which product it belongs to — the user must hold 'top bar = left/Sea Salt Chips' in memory across all four cards (Overall, Nutrition, Additives, Processing).
  - **Why:** Yuka's and Ivy's per-factor compare rows always tag each bar/row with the item it represents; an unlabeled two-bar stack forces working-memory mapping and defeats the whole purpose of a compare screen. Fails the 'strong typographic hierarchy / one obvious read' bar.
  - **Fix:** In metricBlock, give each SharedScaleMeter a leading identity marker — a small product initial/short-name label (or a tiny band swatch keyed to the header) in a fixed 2-line grid so bars still align. E.g. wrap each meter in an HStack with Text(compactLabel(pair.a.name)).font(.caption2).foregroundStyle(Theme.textSecondary) of fixed width, A on top row / B on bottom, matching the header column order. `CompareView.swift:106`
- 🟠 **HIGH · accessibility** — In side-by-side, the round winner is signalled ONLY by a 12%-opacity band-tint wash behind the winning bar (SharedScaleMeter emphasized). There is no winner word. The compact layout DOES add a visible 'Higher' text tag (lines 139-143), so the two layouts are inconsistent and the standard one relies on color alone.
  - **Why:** DESIGN_SYSTEM_V3 §7 and the teardown AVOID list both mandate 'color is never the only signal' (band = color+word+icon). A faint tint is also near-invisible for low-vision users and at a glance. The compact layout already proves the fix exists.
  - **Fix:** Reuse the compact pattern in metricBlock: when row.winner == side, render a small Text("Higher").font(.caption.weight(.bold)).foregroundStyle(band.tint) trailing the winning bar (or as a leading chip). Keep the subtle wash as reinforcement, not the sole signal. `CompareView.swift:108`
- 🟠 **HIGH · hierarchy** — 'Overall' is presented twice with equal weight: as the two ScoreBadge rings in the header AND as the first metric card, styled identically to the three sub-score cards (Nutrition/Additives/Processing). The composite score has no visual dominance over its components.
  - **Why:** Teardown AVOID #4 (badge soup): 'one primary verdict; sub-scores demoted and drillable, never four peer badges.' Yuka/Oasis lead with a single dominant score; here Overall competes with itself and flattens into the sub-score list, weakening the top-level takeaway.
  - **Fix:** Drop the 'Overall' row from the metric cards and let the header ScoreBadge rings be the single overall comparison — OR keep one and elevate it (larger card, display-font number, divider before the three factors). Simplest: in Models.swift ComparePair.rows stop prepending the Overall MetricRow (lines 220-228) so metricRows shows only the three factors. `CompareView.swift:84`
- 🟡 **MEDIUM · interaction** — The two 'Pick this one' CTAs sit at the very bottom of the scroll, after the header and all metric cards, inside the ScrollView. The decision action — the point of the screen — is only reachable after scrolling past everything.
  - **Why:** Teardown interaction cue: 'Sticky bottom pill CTA for one-handed reach.' A comparison screen exists to make a choice; burying both choices below the fold adds friction versus the persistent-action pattern Oasis/Ivy use.
  - **Fix:** Move the pickButton HStack (lines 90-93) out of metricRows and into a .safeAreaInset(edge: .bottom) bar on the outer VStack, with Theme.canvas background + a top hairline (Theme.border) so it floats above the scroll and stays one tap away. `CompareView.swift:90`
- 🟡 **MEDIUM · copy** — The screen never states the overall takeaway in words. The only cue that one product scored higher overall is which CTA is filled green — a subtle, easily-missed signal. There is no calm summary sentence.
  - **Why:** Never-a-dead-end + clarity: users should get the verdict without decoding button styling. Oasis pairs its score with a plain-language word; a neutral one-liner also reinforces the ED-safe 'pick this one, never worse' framing already in the code comments.
  - **Fix:** Add a single neutral summary line under sideBySideHeader (and above metric cards in compact), e.g. Text based on pair.overallWinner: "{name} scores a little higher overall." in .subheadline Theme.textSecondary; render nothing on a .tie. Keep neutral per COPY_DECK (no 'better/worse'). `CompareView.swift:75`

  - **Why:** DESIGN_SYSTEM_V3 type ramp reserves h3/headline (17-18 semibold) for section identity; the compare columns are exactly that. Yuka/Ivy give the compared item name clear prominence. Current sizing undercuts the 'strong typographic hierarchy' goal.
  - **Fix:** Bump headerColumn's name Text (line 168) from .subheadline.weight(.bold) to .headline (17pt semibold) or Space Grotesk h3 per DesignKit, keeping lineLimit(2). Leaves the brand caption below it as the quiet secondary line. `CompareView.swift:168`

## Swaps — "See a better option"
**Top win:** Kill the double green-pill clutter: make the whole SwapCard tappable to view the product and demote "Save to pantry" to a single small icon action — giving each card one obvious primary gesture and restoring green scarcity (the Yuka/Ivy/Oasis bar).

- 🟠 **HIGH · hierarchy** — Each SwapCard renders TWO equal-size pills — a filled greenDeep "View" and an outlined "Save to pantry" (actionsRow, lines 296–327). With 2–3 ranked cards stacked, the screen shows multiple full-green fills competing at once and no single obvious primary action.
  - **Why:** Violates the v3 §2 green-scarcity ratio (~70% neutral / 25% green; green earns attention by being scarce outside the one CTA) and the Yuka/Ivy/Oasis "one obvious primary action per screen" bar. The reference pattern (teardown screnshot 2.webp) is a tappable card body → view, plus ONE small add affordance (FAB/icon), not two peer pills.
  - **Fix:** Wrap infoBlock in a NavigationLink(value: barcode) so the whole card opens the product, and replace actionsRow with a single small "Save to pantry" icon/text button (or a trailing circular add FAB). One green primary gesture per card; Save becomes the quiet secondary. `SwapsView.swift:296`
- 🟠 **HIGH · component** — presentationDetents([.medium, .large]) defaults to .medium (line 61), but each card is tall (thumbnail+score row + chips + why line + two 44pt buttons ≈ 200pt+). At the medium detent barely one card is visible; the ranked better-options list — the entire purpose of the sheet — is cut off below the fold.
  - **Why:** Never-a-dead-end principle #4 and the teardown's "verdict → action" win depend on the user actually SEEING the ranked alternatives. Yuka/Oasis alternative lists present the full ranked set up front; opening half-height on content-dense data hides the payoff.
  - **Fix:** Lead with the large detent for this content-heavy sheet: .presentationDetents([.large, .medium]) (large first) or drop .medium entirely so the ranked swaps are visible on open. `SwapsView.swift:61`
- 🟡 **MEDIUM · spacing** — Swap cards are flat outlined boxes: surfaceCard (DesignKit.swift:86–94) applies only a 1px Theme.border stroke and NO shadow. On the mint canvas the cards read as drawn outlines, not floating surfaces.
