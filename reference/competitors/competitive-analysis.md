## At a glance

| App | Category | UX strength | UX weakness / opening |
|---|---|---|---|
| Yuka | Ingredient rater | Instant, legible verdict; clean scan | Opaque, fear-tinged single score → add transparency + calm |
| Ivy | UPF scanner | Focused niche, rich detail | Long pre-paywall onboarding; score doubts |
| Oasis | Product safety rankings | Independence narrative; free results | Data provenance doubts; duplicates |
| Cal AI | AI calorie tracker | Frictionless photo logging | Inaccuracy + predatory billing; dead-end |
| Bobby Approved | Seed-oil/MAHA | Simple, fast, free | Alarmist "toxic" tone; no credentials |
| Fig | Dietary restriction | Deep filtering | Pay-to-scan-allergens; cross-contact risk |

## The whitespace
No competitor delivers **legible + credible + calm + actionable** together. That combination is our experience differentiator.

## Screenshots (see `reference/screenshots/`)
61 competitor screens captured from three App Store apps — **Oasis, ChemZero, Food Scanner**. Key visual lessons:
- **Oasis** — calm, light onboarding; product detail with green/red ingredient cards, "How scoring works", "Report an issue", "Ingredient Transparency". Confirms our transparency surfaces; we go further with sourced, dose-aware reasoning and a next action (Oasis dead-ends at the verdict).
- **ChemZero** — alarmist seed-oil/"longevity" framing ("Toxic levels of Seed Oils", "Detected Risks – High", red everywhere, "foods that are aging you"). The fear-mongering tone we explicitly avoid; this is our positioning wedge — calm and credible vs. scary.
- **Food Scanner** — nutrient panel with High/Low/Unknown traffic-light dots; legible but binary. Our score scale is ordinal and non-alarmist (clay, not red).
